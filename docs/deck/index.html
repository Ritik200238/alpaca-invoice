The problem

On a public ledger, an invoice tells everyone everything.

Competitors

read your prices.

Suppliers

read who your customers are.

Lenders

read how late you pay.

So businesses invoice off-chain, where nothing is provable. Privacy alone does not fix it: books get audited, taxes get filed, and Japan's qualified-invoice system requires the tax to be correct for each rate. The terms must stay private and the proof must stay public.

What Inkan does

Four operations, one contract.

Create

The seller's terms stay in their private state. The chain records commitments, and the contract refuses tax groups that do not hold.

Pay

The buyer pays in shielded NIGHT inside the transaction that marks the invoice paid. The contract forwards the coin; it never holds it.

Cancel

The seller can cancel a pending invoice. A paid invoice cannot be retracted.

Audit

The seller lets one auditor see chosen fields of one invoice until a date, and nothing else.

Tax rules inside the proof

The contract refuses an invoice whose tax is wrong.

// for each rate group of a qualified invoice (10% standard, 8% reduced) assert tax * 10000 == net * rate // rate in basis points assert (net10 + tax10) + (net8 + tax8) == total

Compact has no division, so the rule is stated as a product. It is exact for every amount: 15,000 at 10% is 1,500, where dividing first, (net / 10000) × rate, gives 1,000. The verifier learns that the groups are consistent and add up, and nothing about the figures.

Shielded settlement

Payment and status change are one transaction.

Buyerproves they hold the committed terms
→
Contractreceives the coin and forwards it in the same call
→
Sellerat the payout address in the committed terms
  • The coin must equal the invoice total, in the invoice's token.
  • It must go to the seller the invoice names; a prover that lies about the terms is refused.
  • Paying after the private due date is refused, against the block's clock.

Selective audit

Show one auditor the chosen fields, and nothing else.

Scope

Nine fields to choose from: amount, tax, due date, buyer and more. Stored as a nine-slot vector, because Compact has no bitwise operators.

Expiry

The grant ends at a date checked against the block, not against a time the caller chose.

Key

Only a hash of the audit key reaches the chain.

One salt per field. If every field shared one salt, disclosing one would help an auditor test guesses against the rest. With a derived salt per field, it does not.

Architecture

Private state on the device. Commitments on the chain.

Interface
Next.js 14, English, Japanese and Chinese. Not yet connected to the Midnight contract; next step, through the Lace wallet.
Private state & witnesses
The seller's secret and the open terms, handed to circuits as witnesses with size and non-zero checks (witnesses.ts)
Compact contract
Compact 0.23, toolchain 0.31.1: createInvoice, payInvoiceShielded, cancelInvoice, setAuditAuthorization; hashes exported as pure circuits so the app computes exactly what the chain does
Proving & network
Local proof server 8.1.0, Midnight.js 4.1.1, wallet SDK 1.2.0, Zswap shielded coins, DUST fees, the public Preview indexer

Identity is a key derived from a secret the caller proves they hold, never ownPublicKey(). Every claimed time is bounded to within one hour of the block and never ahead of it.

Who it is for

Businesses that must prove their books without publishing them.

Sellers

Invoice and get paid on-chain without showing prices or customers to anyone reading the ledger.

Buyers

Pay in the same step that records the payment, with a settlement anchor they can later show an auditor.

Auditors

Check exactly the fields they are shown against commitments the chain has held since the invoice was created.

Why Japan first: the qualified-invoice system makes per-rate tax correctness a legal requirement, and that is a rule a proof can check. Later, credit proofs from settled invoices let a seller show a lender a payment record without the invoices behind it. Pricing is not set yet.
